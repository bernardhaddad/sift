Who actually makes money (verified ledger)

Written 2026-09-02, revised the same day after the full eight-lens sweep and adversarial verification completed. Companion to five-profit-classes.md. That document is the framework; this one is the receipts: every dollar figure below is tied to a named primary or near-primary source, with a verification status, a "can a $0 solo operator do this" verdict, and a legality note. Read Coverage and limits before quoting anything.

How to read the verification column. Each lens's top claims were handed to two independent refuter agents with the instruction to break them. "Verified" means both refuters re-derived the numbers from the cited source and at least one independent source and could not refute. "Verified, corrected" means the numbers held but a framing or detail was wrong, and the corrected reading is what appears here. "Refuted" means a headline framing failed. "Not verified" means the claim sat in the lower-confidence tier that the refuters did not reach, or the refuters did not run.

Three readings of that table matter more than any single row:

  1. Money flows up the stack, not sideways. The fee-taking layers (rows 1, 2, 3, 6) together booked on the order of $2.5–3B across 2024–26. Every verified individual trading fortune combined (rows 9, 10, 15 and the >$1M wallet tier) is an order of magnitude smaller. The fee-takers earn on losing trades as much as winning ones.
  2. The high-win-rate "traders" are not trading. The 87% hit rate belongs to deployers buying their own launch; the $627K-from-$285 wallet was internal; LIBRA's and MELANIA's winners had the tweet timing. The best legitimate trader on record with a human-speed method (Gake) runs a 42% win rate and makes it up in position size.
  3. The refuters mostly confirmed the numbers and cut the framing. Of 24 claims that went through both refuters, 19 survived and 5 were refuted. Every refutation was a superlative, a generalization, or a mislabel; not one headline number was found wrong.

3. MEV, snipers and the latency stack

Sandwich MEV. Ghostlogs at Solana Accelerate (May 2025): $370–500M extracted Jan 2024–May 2025 across 8.5B trades. Verified against the Ghostlogs report itself; the $370M lower bound is SOL/stablecoin revenue and the $500M upper bound adds memecoin-denominated revenue. Helius confirms one validator-run program (DeezNode's "Vpe") executed 1.55M sandwiches in 30 days (Dec 2024–Jan 2025) for 65,880 SOL (~$13.4M), nearly half of all sandwiches; 16 of the top 20 sandwiched tokens were Pump.fun mints. Extraction has since fallen by roughly an order of magnitude, and Jito, Marinade and the Solana Foundation blacklist validators for it. A separate "protection money" thesis (users overpaying Jito tips to avoid MEV) was refuted: the IMC '25 paper's $2.4M defensive spend over four months is real but under 1% of the ~$300M of Jito tips in the same window, the median defensive tip is the floor rather than an overpayment, and JIP-24 routes 100% of Jito's 6% block-engine fee to the DAO, not 100% of tips.

Deployer-funded sniping (Pine Analytics, Apr 2025, verified via BeInCrypto, Bitget and a full Wu Blockchain translation): "over 50% of tokens are sniped in the genesis block." Filtering for wallets funded directly by the deployer: 15,000+ launches in one month (~1.75% of issuance, a lower bound), 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers, >15,000 SOL net realized, 87% of snipes profitable, 55% sold within one minute, 85% within five, activity concentrated 14:00–23:00 UTC. The documented case used 1.2 SOL across three wallets. A 2026 arXiv replication (2607.02795, 166K launches) still finds 1,012 persistent co-firing wallet cohorts.

Non-insider sniping (not verified; every figure comes from vendors selling the stack): slot 0 vs slot 2 is "20–60% of the upside"; co-located Frankfurt bots land slot 0 ~89% of the time vs <20% remote; "roughly 90% of DIY sniper bots fail"; minimum credible stack $250–$700/month, realistically $1,000–$2,500/month before tips. ISSTA 2025 (2.9B transactions): bot accounts fail 58.4% of transactions vs 6.2% for humans, and every failure burns priority fees.

Bundling your own launch (not verified): 1.5–4 SOL minimum with free open-source tooling. It is the "insider allocation" fact pattern on which wire-fraud, illegal-gambling and money-transmission claims against Pump.fun's parent survived dismissal on Sept 1 2026 (Aguilar v. Baton, SDNY). SEC staff said memecoins are not securities (Feb 27 2025); the Oct 2024 DOJ/SEC Massachusetts case (14 charged) was for wash trading.

7. Copy-trading

The most thoroughly verified negative result in the sweep.

  • On-chain, peer-reviewed (Luo, Feng, Xu, Liu, ACM Web Conference 2026, arXiv 2601.08641; both refuters downloaded and grepped the paper): 6,000 Pump.fun tokens with full wallet histories. Every conventional wallet-selection model (LASSO, neural net, XGBoost, zero-shot LLM) produced negative copier returns even when smart money was identified correctly, because of the "imitation penalty" (buying after the leader on the bonding curve, 1% fee, exiting into worse liquidity). The best-case multi-agent pipeline got copiers ~3% per trade vs 14% for the copied wallet. Bundle bots were present in ~25% of coins and sniper bots in the majority; the paper describes adversaries "concealing exposure across multiple wallets" and "coordinated exit using copier liquidity."
  • Centralized exchanges (YieldFund, Nov 2025, 100,236 outcomes across 1,486 leaders on Binance, Bybit, MEXC; figures verified): only 43.6% of leaders produced positive PnL for their followers; 48.5% of follower outcomes were green; MEXC followers were net −210,040 USDT. Correction: the "97% of leaders green" figure is an artifact of leaderboard eligibility rules (Bybit requires positive 7/30/90-day returns to be listed), not evidence about leaders in general.
  • Cented at a 10-second lag: −21.3% in a cookin.fun simulation (not verified).
  • Leaders farm copiers (not verified): copy-trade guides now screen out wallets that sell 15–20% of trades within 5 seconds because they "buy a token, let their followers copy the trade, then sell within seconds." A documented leaderboard wallet showed +$247K realized (85% win rate) while carrying −$489K unrealized.
  • Cost schedule (GMGN docs; not verified): 30–35% slippage recommended, 50%+ on new coins, 0.006–0.01 SOL priority fees, failed transactions still burn fees.
  • KOL wallet population (MadeOnSol, 1.6M trades across 1,058 tracked wallets; not verified): median win rate 57.1%; KOLs buy 1.5x more often than they sell but sell in bigger size (avg buy 1.52 SOL, avg sell 2.24 SOL); 9,404 coordinated cluster events averaging 5.1 KOLs. The tracker's own conclusion: "following every trade from a popular KOL is a losing strategy."
  • Who wins: the terminals. DefiLlama re-pulled by the refuters: Axiom $27.5M in the 30 days to Aug 14 2026, GMGN $25.0M; today Axiom $40.3M/30d (39.5% of the category), GMGN $34.2M (33.5%), fomo $16.4M. OdinBot (not verified) generated $1.6M revenue on $250M volume before Cielo acquired it in July 2026.

8. KOLs, promoters and alpha groups

The money map has two flows, and the smaller one is the fee.

  • Fees (ZachXBT leak, Sept 1 2025; verified against ZachXBT's own post, The Block, Cybernews): one project solicited 200+ accounts, 160+ accepted, rates ran $50 to $60,000 (the $60K account said it covered ongoing marketing work, not one post), and fewer than five disclosed the content as paid. Correction: this is one campaign, not a market survey, so "95% of all KOLs don't disclose" does not follow. Leaked wallets were later traced to accounts linked to the ~$143M CR7 rug.
  • Supply is the larger flow. Crypto Beast promoted $ALT, then 45+ wallets funded from his own address dumped ~$11M on July 14 2025, taking the cap from $190M to $3M in about an hour (verified; the "largest 2025 case" superlative was refuted since LIBRA was larger). Sahil Arora's celebrity factory (not verified): 40+ addresses, $2–3M (ZachXBT) to $26M (Bubblemaps), celebrities paid flat fees to tweet a contract. KOL rounds (not verified): present in ~75% of notable TGEs, 10–30% unlocked at listing; rare 21x tranches (Aster) against "over ten KOL rounds last year, none were profitable." The 2026 $ANSEM episode: a dev gifted 65% of supply to a ~1M-follower account.
  • What followers get (HBS / Review of Accounting Studies, 36K tweets by 180 influencers; verified verbatim): +1.83% on day one, −2.2% at day 10, −6.5% at day 30, −19% at three months, worse for self-described experts and small caps.   • Beyond memecoins (not verified): Polymarket's CMO paid $2.5M through a personal PayPal to 800+ recipients, at least $350K of it to ~two dozen creators for ~490 undisclosed posts (POLITICO, June 2026). FTC has named social-media advertising a 2026 enforcement priority; five H1 2025 class actions over hidden influencer deals sought $1.1B.

Bot referral kickbacks: Trojan 25% at level 1 plus four deeper levels, BONKbot 30%/20%/10% by month, GMGN and Maestro 25%. Realistic math from the lens: 100 subscribers at $50 is $5K/month gross; a follower trading $10K/month through your Trojan link yields you about $25.

9. Narrative and news plays

This is the lens closest to this project's thesis that narrative is what matters, so its verification gap (two of three top claims did not get refuters) is the one to close first.

The documented event winners split into three tiers:

  1. Insider-dominated launches (LIBRA, MELANIA, YZY, ZREAL): pre-announcement or same-block wallets captured $6M–$100M+ per event and 74–95% of public buyers lost. All were detectable after the fact by pre-announcement buys, 87–94% single-wallet supply concentration, and deployer-funded sniper clusters. LIBRA/MELANIA verified; YZY and ZREAL not.
  2. Elite-capital snipers on hyped public launches (TRUMP): not team insiders, but seven-figure bankrolls, bots, and $84K in priority fees for a first-second fill. Not verified.
  3. Genuine non-insider profits on breaking-news coins (not verified): Charlie Kirk tokens (top five traders >$1.6M combined; creators $563K in fees in 24 hours; one token to $74.9M cap in four hours; ~700 fresh addresses simulating demand), Pope Leo tokens ($60–74M cap within 24 hours), GHIBLI ($21M cap in 20 hours, retail realized profits up to ~$26K, then $39M → $3.4M in ten days), CZ's "4" ($3K → $2M for one Lookonchain-tracked wallet, then −92.8%), PENGUIN ($54K → $1.7M for a wallet that had a <15% win rate across 1,000+ tokens and −$1.3M cumulative before the trade).

The window is the finding. Across every tier-3 case the pattern is the same: entry within minutes to a few hours of the public trigger, peak within 4–24 hours, then −85% to −95% inside one to two weeks. The documented big winners are one wallet per event, highlighted by Lookonchain; the typical reactor's realized gain was in the $26K-and-under range and the same tokens carried heavy sniper and bundle activity. The most reliable income in every case went to deployers (creator fees) and infrastructure.

The five profit classes

Written 2026-08-31. Who actually makes money in memecoins, how each class extracts it, what it costs to join, and how our system detects or defends against each. Every claim is sourced either to published data or to this project's own measured ledger (500+ hourly cycles, 4 pre-registered strategies, ~90 closed paper trades, 70K+ panel observations).

Class 1 — The House: deployers, insiders, block-0 snipers

Who. The people who create the token, their allied wallets, and the bots that buy in the same block the pool opens.

How the money is made. The house profits from allocation, not prediction:

  • Bundled launches: the deployer funds dozens of fresh wallets before launch and buys its own token in the deploy transaction bundle. Retail sees "organic" early buyers; the cluster holds a controlling share it sells into every wave of demand. Our XST autopsy: RugCheck-clean on the surface, ~74% held by one fragmented cluster underneath — it stopped our M2 book out within one hour of entry.
  • Block-0 sniping: bots watch the deploy mempool and buy in the first block, ahead of any human. Our research corpus: >50% of launches are sniped at block 0. The professionalization is real — production sniper stacks get cost-per-win down to ~$2.65 vs ~$7.75 for amateurs, with Jito bundle tips of 0.01–0.05 SOL as the pay-to-win lever.
  • Serial relaunch factories: when a narrative hits, the same operators launch the same coin daily. We logged ~25 GTA6-named and ~20 MarsCoin-named pools in two weeks; nearly all printed fake 8-figure volumes on ~zero tracked liquidity and died within 48h — each iteration harvesting a fresh cohort.

Unit economics. Deploying costs ~$2 on Pump.fun. A successful extraction cycle returns thousands to millions. The risk isn't market risk — it's competition from other snipers and the (thin) legal exposure.

Defense (ours, working). Never touch <24h pools; require locked LP ≥80%, top-10 holders ≤30%, insider flags ≤2; treat many same-name pools as disqualifying; check bubblemaps-style clustering when a chart looks too clean. This class is why our rug gate exists, and it has eaten every rug thrown at it since Amendment 1.

Joinable? Only by becoming the predator — bundling and sniping retail. Not our game, on ethics and on arms-race economics both.

Class 2 — The Shovel-Sellers: platforms, bots, signal-sellers

Who. Pump.fun, the DEXes, trading bots (1%/side), RPC providers, launch-bundler tools, paid alpha groups.

How the money is made. A percentage of every trade, win or lose. This is the only class with a positive edge on 100% of volume:

  • Pump.fun alone made $124.7M in fees in Q1 2026 — 36% of all Solana app revenue, the network's single largest earner. (Even in the June downturn its model still prints; volume risk, not direction risk.)
  • Trading bots charge ~1% per side; add 0.3% DEX fees and slippage and you get the 3% round-trip cost we model on every trade. In our M2 book, cost drag alone accounted for roughly half the book's entire deficit — money transferred to this class regardless of trade quality.
  • Signal-sellers and promo middlemen: crypto marketing runs $200 to $500K+ per KOL post, with a lean memecoin launch spending $25K–$60K over six weeks on promotion. Every dollar of that is Class-2 revenue before a single trade happens.

Unit economics. Near-zero marginal cost, revenue proportional to mania. The gold-rush constant: the shovel-seller outlives every gold vein.

Defense (ours, working). Model the 3% honestly on every paper trade (many "profitable" backtests die on this line alone); require any entry signal's median move to clear costs (the dip-zone gate exists precisely because it was the only band beating +3%).

Joinable? Legitimately, yes — this is the one class where skills transfer honestly: build tooling, dashboards, screeners, research. It's a business-building path, not a trading path, and the only class where the customer isn't necessarily a victim.

Class 3 — The KOLs: attention sellers

Who. Influencers and call-channel operators paid to point audiences at tokens.

How the money is made. Three stacked streams: (1) promotion fees ($200–$500K+ per post; tier-1 effective CPM $40–100); (2) token allocations — 90% of $10K+ KOLs now demand tokens instead of cash, which means they are structurally pre-loaded to dump on their own audience; (3) front-running their own calls.

The evidence it's extraction, not alpha. 86% of KOL-promoted coins drop 90%+ within a quarter (377-KOL study in our corpus). Our own ledger is a controlled experiment on this: six trades entered while the token carried call-channel mentions — five full stops (−1.12R each: TOAD, EYE, CVXV666, cc, GTAVI) and one early-narrative outlier win (CYBERLEEK +5.85R), net ≈ +0.25R before variance. Being mentioned is a distribution signal: someone paid to create your exit urge.

Defense (ours, working). The quiet-channels gate: zero call-channel mentions at entry (M3/M4), KOL-heat exits on held positions, and the mention log as a veto stream. The constant 1-mention-per-cycle drip we see on certain tokens is the fingerprint of paid rotation, and the system reads it as poison, correctly.

Joinable? Only by monetizing an audience you'd be dumping on. No.

Class 4 — The Elite On-Chain Traders: the 2%

Who. The small cohort that genuinely trades its way to profit. Per current Dune data, fewer than 2% of memecoin participants ever clear $10K in profit. April 2026 Pump.fun data: 73% of wallets showed gains, but 65% of winners made under $500 and only 5.4% cleared $1,000 — the "most wallets win" headline hides how tiny the wins are.

How the money is made. The documented big winners (e.g., $14.8M on TRUMP from a $31M position; $17M on WIF from $6M) share a repeatable profile:

  1. Capital — positions in the millions, so a 75% move is life-changing rather than lunch money, and slippage/fees are proportionally tiny.
  2. Infrastructure — private RPCs, wallet-cluster tracking, sub-second execution, bundle detection. They see the Class-1 house forming and either front-run it or refuse the trade.
  3. Full-time screen hours — narrative windows open and close in minutes.
  4. Mechanical profit-taking — systematically harvesting 10x moves rather than praying for 1000x; capped losses, uncapped-but-harvested winners; surviving to trade again is the whole strategy.

The part people miss. Points 1–3 are money. Point 4 is discipline — and it's the same shape our mined evidence produced independently: enter weakness not strength, cap every loss, let winners run, refuse hyped entries. The elite do at second-speed with capital what our system does at hour-speed on paper.

Joinable? Progressively. Point 4 costs nothing and we already run it. Points 1–3 cost real money (private infra starts at ~$100–500/month; meaningful capital is the real gate). The pre-registered PROMOTE gate (≥60 closed trades, positive R, t ≥ 1.645) is our bridge: it's the evidence threshold at which putting the first small real capital behind a strategy stops being gambling.

Class 5 — Retail at Human Speed: the exit liquidity

Who. Everyone trading public signals (trending lists, KOL calls, green candles) at human reaction time. Statistically, the counterparty to Classes 1–3.

How the money is lost. Every mechanism above needs a buyer: the house needs someone to snipe in front of, the shovel-sellers need volume, the KOLs need an audience that buys the call. Retail at hourly-or-slower speed is that someone. The base rates: 80% of memecoins dead within 48h, ~4.6% surviving 90 days, median pool in our own deep-liquidity universe losing 8.8%/day.

Our measurement of this tier is the honest headline of this project: four pre-registered strategies, 500+ live cycles, every filter we could mine — −20R cumulative, of which M1 (naive momentum chasing, the most "retail" strategy) lost −13.5R on its own before its kill gate stopped it. It cost $0 to learn what most people pay thousands to learn, one stop-loss at a time.

The exit from Class 5 is not a secret indicator. It's (a) refusing Classes 1–3 their revenue — the audit checklist, the quiet-channels rule, the cost model; and (b) either building Class-2 assets or earning Class-4 membership through the evidence gate. There is no third door, and anyone selling one is Class 3.

The aged band: beta, not alpha

The last lever with a measured reason to exist. research-cost.md found that pools 30 days and older cost 0.23% to trade against 3–8% for M2's band, and mining-report-2.md found that the only zone in the panel that did not lose was "aged, deep and boring", where the 3% cost model ate the whole return. The open question was whether any aged cell is positive at its real cost. This note answers it on the same 14-day panel (2026-08-22 to 09-05, 31,539 observations with true forward returns from each pool's own candles, art545/obs.json from scripts/mine2.py), re-scored at a 0.5% round trip, with the two checks the earlier passes lacked: statistics clustered by token-day, and returns measured in excess of the aged universe at the same snapshot.

Verdict

Null. The aged dip cell is positive at its real cost, and the whole of that return is market drift. SOL rose 10.8% over the panel. The aged universe returned +3.3% at 48h. The dip cell returned +3.5%. Its excess over its own universe at the same snapshots is +0.3% with a t-statistic of 0.3, and in the second week the excess is −2.5%. Every boundary, liquidity floor and sub-cell tested behaves the same way. Nothing here supports a registration.

What was measured

Memecoin/SOL pairs, age over 30 days (720h), liquidity ≥ $250K: 9,189 observations on 81 tokens. Returns are 24h and 48h forward from the snapshot's hourly close, capped at +300%, less a 0.5% round trip (the measured aged median is 0.23%, the mean 0.33%, the worst of ten 0.79%). t is computed across token-day clusters (each token's observations within one calendar day averaged first), which removes the hourly resampling of the same position that inflated every earlier panel statistic. excess is the same return minus the mean return of the whole aged universe at the same snapshot, which removes the market's move. A and B are the first and second week.

  • The aged band was the last lever with a measured reason to exist, and it is beta. The cost advantage is real (a 14x cheaper round trip) and it turns a losing cell into a break-even one; it does not turn it into an edge. Buying aged memecoins is a leveraged, less liquid way of holding SOL.

What a round trip really costs

The cost model was the largest untested term in the project (research-levers.md). Every lever varied entries or exits while holding COST_PER_SIDE = 0.015 frozen, then reported effects of ±0.03 to 0.05R. This measures the term itself against live router quotes rather than assuming it. The result is decisive and it is not the direction the hypothesis predicted.

Method

Jupiter v1 quote API, exact-in, both legs. For each mint: quote SOL → token for a $100-equivalent amount, take the exact outAmount, quote it straight back to SOL. The round trip is 1 − (SOL_out / SOL_in), which captures pool fees, route spread and price impact together, with no modelling. Priority fee and any platform fee sit on top of every number here.

What it means for M2

paper.py documents its 3% as "1% bot fee + 0.3% DEX fee + base slippage". The 1% is a platform fee and was the one part thought to be a procurement decision. It is not the problem. The market cost alone is 7.9%, and a platform fee would sit on top.

M2's entry universe breaks even at roughly a 1.05% round trip (research-levers.md). Actual is 7.9%. The gap is about sevenfold. On that document's own sensitivity of ≈0.04R per 1% of cost, moving 3% → 7.9% costs a further 0.20R per trade, taking the universe from −0.077R to roughly −0.28R (−$7 per $100 trade).

This closes the question the eight levers were asking. Every one of them searched for an effect worth 0.005–0.10R. The cost error alone is 0.20R and the total cost term is 0.32R. No entry filter or exit rule can pay a 7.9% toll. An edge would have to be worth more than 7.9% per trade before it earned a cent, and nothing in this project has found one worth 1%.

The one direction this opens

The eight levers all varied entries and exits inside the 24h–14d band, holding the band fixed. Nobody tested moving the band, and it is the term that dominates everything else.

The exit study already reported that its best sample was the aged one — 7–30 days old, liquidity ≥$250K — at −0.019R per trade with a 50% stop, which is the closest any sample in this project has come to breakeven. That was computed at the modelled 3% cost. If the real cost on that band is nearer 0.3%, the same sensitivity puts it at roughly +0.09R per trade.

That is arithmetic across two separate measurements, not a result. It is stated here as the next hypothesis, and it gets the same treatment as everything else before anything is built on it: measured on the panel, gate-matched control, both halves, top-name removal, and the adversarial pass. What makes it worth testing ahead of everything else is that it is the only proposal in the corpus that moves the largest term rather than a 0.05R one.

What changes

  • M2 is not amended. It is at 46 of 60 closed trades. This is a measurement of the world, not a defect in the code, and the registered rules run to their verdict.
  • The §12.1 review inherits a hard number. STRATEGY.md §12.5 promised the modelled 3% would be replaced after 30 live fills. It can be replaced now, with quotes, and the honest figure is 7.9% for this universe. Any promotion arithmetic that uses 3% is wrong.
  • Any successor states its cost from router quotes on its own universe before its first trade, and re-quotes when the universe definition changes, because the cost is a property of the age band, not a constant.
  • No live tranche. At 7.9% round trip the §12.1 preconditions cannot be met by any book trading this age band.

Limits

  • Seven fully-gated pools, ten per age band, five established tokens, all quoted once on one afternoon. Fee schedules are dynamic and decay, so a single snapshot understates how much the number moves.
  • Jupiter quotes are indicative, not fills. A real order adds priority fee, failure-and-retry, and MEV, all of which push the same direction.
  • The 14–30d band returned exactly one qualifying pool, so the middle of the age curve is unmeasured. The comparison is 24h–14d against 30d+ with a gap between them.
  • The hand-picked established tokens are a survivor set; the fourth control replaces them with scanner-drawn pools and is the version to cite. Neither answers what happens to a token that reaches 30 days without becoming famous and then gets traded, which is what the successor hypothesis actually requires.
  • None of this has been through the adversarial pass that the lever study used.

Fill conventions and phantom survivors

The paper cycle quotes each held pool once an hour and compares that single price to the stop level. A dip that breaks the stop and recovers inside the hour is invisible to it. A live account with a resting stop-loss order does not get that reprieve: it is filled.

The fix (Amendment 7)

  • data.pool_low_since(http, address, since_ts) — lowest intra-hour low since a position was last checked. One OHLCV call per open position, at most 8 per cycle, fail-soft.
  • run.add_intracycle_lows() — attaches it to the quote for every open position.
  • paper.manage() — fires the stop when the quote or that low breaches the stop level, filling at the stop level. When no low is available the quoted price decides alone, exactly as before: missing data is never a free pass in either direction.

This can only make the books worse. It was applied mid-experiment anyway, because the alternative is carrying a known-optimistic verdict to trade 60 and putting real money behind a measurement artifact. STRATEGY §10 records that trades closed before 2026-09-06 were measured under the old model and that the §12.1 review must say how many of the counted 60 predate the amendment.

What it does not change

  • It is not a strategy defect. The entries and exits are what they were; only their measurement is now honest.
  • It does not touch the entry work. The two mining passes found no edge and still find none; this makes the measured expectancy worse, not better.
  • It cuts the same way as the exit study. A wider stop is now doubly indicated: it is further from the noise band, so fewer ordinary dips reach it at all. With a 40% stop ZCAT survives its wick.

Prompted by the owner's question, "how to adjust this so it is not in losses". What is NOT true

Two things I checked because they looked true in a naive grid search and were not:

  • The trail is not the defect. Varying only the trail (15 / 25 / 35 / 50 / no trail) with the stop held at 25% is flat-to-worse in all four samples. The gains in a full-grid search came from the rows that also widened the stop.

What it does not fix

Every cell above is still negative. The best result in the table, a 50% stop on the aged band, is −0.019R per trade. Correcting the stop takes expectancy from clearly negative to slightly negative; it removes a self-inflicted loss, it does not create an edge. Combined with mining-report.md and mining-report-2.md, this is the third independent line of evidence that there is no edge in this universe at hourly cadence with public data.

There is also a live-fill caveat: 95% of young-pool observations touch −30% on a candle wick within 24h while only 7% close there (mining-report-2.md). A wider stop is more exposed to wick fills on a live venue than the paper book's hourly-close fills suggest, so the measured improvement is an upper bound on what a live book would capture.

What changes

  • M2 is not amended. It is at 39 of the 60 closed trades its verdict needs. Changing the stop mid-experiment would make those 39 trades incomparable to the next 21 and would breach the pre-registration in §4. The book runs to its verdict on the rules it was registered with. This is a cost, and it is the point of pre-registering.
  • No successor is registered either. A successor with a corrected stop would still have negative measured expectancy, because the entries carry no edge. Registering one would be motion, not evidence.
  • The finding is binding on any future book. Any experiment registered after this date states its stop against measured volatility of its own universe, and no stop inside the ordinary daily range of the tokens it trades is acceptable. If M2 reaches its verdict and a successor is ever justified by a real entry edge, it starts at a 40–50% stop with proportionally smaller notional so dollar risk is unchanged.
  • The live tranche's sizing rule (§12.2) is reviewed against this before any funding. It currently inherits the book's stop; on this evidence a promoted book's live stop must be re-derived, not inherited.

Limits

  • 14 days, one regime, and only pools with candle history. Pseudo-entries are deduplicated to one per pool per day so a single pool cannot dominate, which is why the samples are 45–116 entries rather than thousands.
  • The four samples overlap: M2's own trades are a subset of its universe. They are not four independent experiments, they are four partitions that happen to agree.
  • Re-run after the 45-day panel has filled (mid-October) before treating any number here as settled.

Mining pass 2: true forward returns

Run with scripts/mine2.py (fetch candles, then report). Panel: the hourly market log from 2026-08-22 to 2026-09-05, 1,688 of 1,775 pools with GeckoTerminal hourly candles, 31,539 observations with a measured 24h outcome, 16,708 of them memecoin/SOL pairs. Verdict

Null. No feature, band, or grid cell clears the 3% round-trip cost on a basis that survives the two checks that matter: the second week, and more than one token.

  1. The market's base rate is slightly negative after costs. Memecoin/SOL pairs return a median −0.7% over 24h (capped mean +1.8%, net −1.2%), win 30% of the time, and 22% touch −30% within a day on candle lows. Week A net −3.8%, week B +3.8%; the swing is a handful of week-B pumps.
  2. The 1–14 day universe that M1 and M2 trade is the worst place in the panel. Net −13.8% at 24h, median −18.8%, across 39 pools, negative in both weeks. This is the measured reason those books lost, independent of the entry rule laid on top.
  3. Every band that looks positive is one token. ZCAT carries 100% of the positive sum in the M1/M2 momentum band, STONK 99–100% of M4's, CATE 100% of the crash-bounce bands. Those are hourly re-observations of a single event, not a repeated pattern. With the top token removed each of these bands is negative.
  4. The M4 band does not replicate. 7–30d, ≥$250K liquidity, dip zone, balanced flow: week A net +12%, week B −33%, 8 pools in total. The dropout-based finding of the first pass was a survivorship artifact of scoring leaderboard exits as death; with real candles the "quiet survivor" premise is not supported.
  5. The sniper zone (<24h) is where the tails are, and it is untradeable for us. Capped mean +33% but median −33%, 85% touch −30%, and the largest prints are on $0–4K liquidity pools or single-candle artifacts (SOLCAT +3,000x on $1K liquidity). This is the verified insider/deployer zone, excluded by design.
  6. The only zone that does not lose is aged, deep, and boring: >30 days, ≥$250K liquidity, any 24h change. Net ≈ −2% (gross ≈ +1%), 1% stop rate, spread over 90 pools. It has no edge, only low variance; it is where the cost model eats the whole return.

What changes

  • No M5 pre-registration. Nothing here meets the bar (out-of-sample positive, many pools, clears 3%). Registering an experiment on a one-token band would be curve-fitting.
  • The market log retention goes from 14 to 45 days (scripts/run.py), so the next pass has three times the independent tokens. The cache cost is ~10 MB compressed.
  • Crash-bounce is logged as a hypothesis, not a signal. CATE's ~+90% bounce after a −44% day across three pools is one event; the other 11 crash-bounce tokens returned a median of about −7%. Re-test when ≥30 distinct tokens are in the band.
  • Stop-hit rates in young pools are wick-driven. 95% of 1–14d observations touch −30% on candle lows within 24h, but only 7% close there. Paper fills at hourly closes, so the books see the 7%; live fills would see the 95%. This widens the paper-to-live gap the live-tranche review (STRATEGY §12) must measure before any real money.

Audit checklist

The checklist our system runs automatically every hour, written so a human can run it manually on any coin in ~5 minutes. Every rule cites something that actually happened in our live logs. Order matters — each step is cheaper than the next; stop at the first FAIL.

Step 0 — Identify the REAL pool (30 seconds)

Search the ticker on GeckoTerminal or DexScreener. If you see many pools with the same name, that alone is a warning: hot narratives spawn serial relaunch factories. Live evidence: ~25 "GTA6/GTA" pools and ~20 "MarsCoin" pools in 14 days; nearly all died within 48h. Pick the pool with the highest locked liquidity and the longest history — audit that one and ignore the clones. If you can't tell which is "real", the answer is none.

Step 1 — Liquidity honesty (1 minute)

  • Liquidity ≥ $250K for anything you might hold longer than minutes. Dropout risk in our panel falls monotonically with liquidity (91% → 6%).
  • Volume/liquidity ratio between 0.5 and 10. A pool claiming $10M+ daily volume on ~$0 tracked liquidity is wash trading, full stop. Live evidence: MarsCoin clones printing $15–20M "volume" and a fake +11,865,752% price with zero liquidity.
  • LP locked ≥ 80% (RugCheck shows this). Unlocked LP = the deployer can drain the pool. Live evidence: a GTA clone drained $23K → $1K liquidity, price −94%, in one day.

Step 2 — Holder structure (1 minute, rugcheck.xyz)

  • RugCheck normalized score ≤ 30.
  • Top-10 holders ≤ 30% of supply.
  • ≤ 2 insider-flagged wallets, insider supply ≤ 10%.
  • Holder count matches the story: a "viral" coin with 400 holders is bots.
  • No data = no entry. If the tools can't see the token, neither can you.
  • Funding clusters ≤ 10% of supply. The blind spot of per-wallet flags is the fragmented bundle: XST passed naive RugCheck while Bubblemaps showed ~74% held by one cluster of small wallets; it stopped us out within an hour. Three weeks later the ring was still visible on-chain: 17 of its top-20 holders were funded through one-use throwaway wallets from one hub and received their tokens by direct transfer from one distributor. Since Amendment 6 the system traces every top-20 holder two funding hops back and to the wallet that sent it its first tokens, and refuses a cluster over 10% or clusters over 20% combined. By hand: bubblemaps.io; treat wallets that share a funder, or that received supply from the same wallet instead of buying it, as one holder.

Step 3 — Age and lifecycle (1 minute)

  • Pools < 7 days old are lottery tickets: 71–80% vanished within our observation window. Age 7–30 days with surviving liquidity is where holdable coins live.
  • Where is price vs its all-time high? Buying within 25% of ATH is buying someone else's exit. Our only positive-median pocket: 25–60% below ATH, in a modest 24h dip (−15%…−2%), with a higher-low base forming and volume not collapsing.
  • Chart printed one green candle straight up? That's the pump. You are the dump.

Step 4 — Who is telling you about it? (1 minute)

  • Search the ticker in Telegram call channels (t.me/s/<channel> works without an account). If call channels are already shilling it, you are the exit liquidity — being mentioned is a SELL signal, not a buy signal. Live evidence: TOAD entered with 2 KOL mentions → full-stop loss in a day; XST was shilled while being a bundled rug; the same channels' calls were behind M1's −13.5R death.
  • A constant 1-mention-per-cycle drip across days = paid promotion, not organic interest.
  • News correlation cuts the other way too: real news attaches to fake coins. The CYBERLEEK token launched within hours of the real GTA6 leak and failed the rug screen; OnlyMarms copycats multiplied after the real charity coin went viral. A true story does not make the tokens riding it real.

Step 5 — Only now think about profit

Auditing keeps you from losing to fraud; it does not make you money by itself. Profit additionally requires:

  • An entry edge that beats ~3% round-trip costs. In 340h of our own data the only band clearing that bar: deep-liquidity survivors in a modest dip with non-dump flow (median +8.5% next 24h, n=11 — being validated live by experiment M4).
  • Uncapped winners, capped losers: always-on trailing stop (−30%), never add to losers, never average down, time-stop stale positions.
  • Position sizing that survives being wrong: flat small size per trade; the verdict gates (kill at −10R, promote only on statistically positive results over 60+ trades) decide whether the strategy deserves real money — not feelings.

The one-line version

Real pool → honest liquidity → clean holders → right lifecycle phase → nobody shilling it → entry only inside a measured edge, with exits decided in advance. Any step fails → walk away; there will be another coin in an hour. There always is.

How Sift is built

Added 2026-09-05. The free-tool path from who-makes-money-verified.md, "What this means for you at $0", item 3.

What it is

A static, public, hourly-updated Solana memecoin screener built from the data this project already collects: the pool panel, the funding-cluster verdicts (Amendment 6), the value-based trade flow (§8.1), Telegram call-channel heat, and the paper books themselves. Every row carries "trade on GMGN / Axiom / Photon / Jupiter" buttons. When those links carry a referral code, the terminal pays a share of its own fee on every trade the referred wallet makes, for as long as that wallet keeps trading. The visitor pays the same fee either way.

The research ranked this above every trading path because the fee layer earns on volume, not on edge, and the paper books have measured the edge available to us at zero budget as negative. The caveat the refuters attached still stands: no independent builder disclosed revenue, so this is the best-evidenced path, not a guaranteed one.

What Sift refuses to be: a call channel, a copy-trading feed, or a launchpad. Those are the verified-negative paths. It shows data and links to venues; it never says "buy".

One-time setup (three steps, about five minutes)

GitHub Pages cannot be enabled on this private repository on the free plan (the earlier MAYFLY deploy failed on exactly that: "Create Pages site failed: Resource not accessible by integration"). The site therefore lives in a separate public repository that the private workflow pushes into.

  1. Create the public repo bernardhaddad/sift on GitHub (public, initialise with a README). The workflow syncs everything in site/ into it and leaves the README alone.
  2. Enable Pages in that repo: Settings → Pages → Source "Deploy from a branch" → branch main, folder / (root). The site will be at https://bernardhaddad.github.io/sift/.
  3. Create a fine-grained personal access token (GitHub Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained): repository access "Only select repositories" → sift; permissions → Contents: Read and write. Add it to ClaudeAI as the Actions secret SITE_DEPLOY_TOKEN.

Then run the "publish sift site" workflow once by hand (Actions → publish sift site → Run workflow), or wait for the next paper cycle. Until the secret exists the workflow builds the snapshot and skips the push, so nothing fails and nothing is exposed.

What is deliberately not on the page

  • No "buy" signals, scores, or rankings by expected return. The screener shows bands the panel mining found survivable and the reasons the paper system refuses tokens.
  • No launches under 24 hours except behind an explicit "sniper zone" filter, with no cluster or flow data (the paper system never traces them).
  • No wallet tracking or copy feed. Verified negative for followers.
  • No sub-hourly data. The cadence is the paper cycle's; the header shows when the snapshot is stale.

Measuring whether it earns

Referral dashboards report referred volume and payouts. Record monthly: unique visitors (GitHub Pages has no analytics; add a privacy-respecting counter only if wanted), referred sign-ups per terminal, referred volume, payout. The research's own bar: the tool is worth continuing if payouts exceed the time cost within a quarter; otherwise it is a free public good that costs nothing to keep running, which is still the correct outcome.

2026-09-16: the paper cycle moved into this repository's public twin

GitHub Free meters Actions minutes only in private repositories, and the 2,000 included minutes per month were used up on 2026-09-16 (the paper cycle alone runs about 48 times a day at roughly five minutes each). Every scheduled cycle in bernardhaddad/ClaudeAI was refused with "spending limit needs to be increased" from 05:55 UTC until the monthly reset. Public repositories are not metered, and bernardhaddad/sift is public because Pages serves the screener from it. So the cycle now runs there: .github/workflows/paper.yml in sift checks this repository out read-only, keeps the book in sift's own Actions cache, runs scripts/run.py cycle, builds the snapshot and commits it straight to main. No deploy token is needed any more; the site workflow here is kept for manual dispatch only.

State continuity. The private repository's cache could not be read from outside and is evicted after seven idle days, so the book was rebuilt from the published record: the last data.json this host produced (seq 198, 00:48 UTC) carries every closed trade of every book with entry, exit, high, reason and R, and the open list was empty at that moment. sift/paper-seed/ holds that rebuild and is copied into data/ on the first run only. Totals, win counts and verdicts match the published figures for all four books. Two things did not survive: entry_liq on historical trades (null), and the raw 45-day market_log panel, which restarts on 09-16. The 14-day gate-passing and flow lists live on in the published snapshot.
