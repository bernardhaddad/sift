How a trace works

  1. Take the token's 20 largest accounts and their owners, and skip pools and programs.
  2. For every holder wallet with under 1,000 transactions, find who paid for its very first transaction, up to two hops back. For every holder, find who sent its token account its first tokens.
  3. Classify each of those wallets. Programs and exchange hot wallets (over 3,000 transactions and 250 SOL) are public and link nobody; every other wallet is private.
  4. Holders that lead back to the same private wallet form a linked group. Its share of supply is what one actor most likely controls. Every link is listed, so you can check it on Solscan.
  5. A link through a private wallet with over 3,000 transactions but a small balance (possibly an exchange withdrawal wallet or a bot) still counts for Sift's own trading, but is not enough for a "High insider risk" verdict here. The same goes for a wallet seen behind top holders of several tokens: Sift's trading treats it as a shared service, while this page still shows the group, flagged, because it can also be one deployer behind several tokens.

The verdicts

    Limits

    • Only the 20 largest accounts are sampled, and wallets with over 1,000 transactions are not traced back to their funder. A clean result is not a safety guarantee.
    • Results are shown while they are current: scanner traces for 24 hours (2 hours if incomplete), requested traces for 7 days, each with its date.
    • A linked group is strong on-chain evidence that one actor holds several wallets. It is not proof of what they will do.
    • The same thresholds decide what Sift's own paper books refuse to trade. Results are updated hourly for the tokens the scanner watches.
    • Nothing here is financial advice. Sift never sells, promotes, or calls tokens.
